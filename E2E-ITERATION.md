## THE ONE OPEN DECISION (P5) — read this before the round log

Everything in P1-P4 and P6 is built, gated and green. P5 has been open since
round 10 because its acceptance criterion cannot be met, not because the work is
unfinished. This is the whole of what an owner needs to rule on.

**The criterion as written:** "reproduce today's DEMO dataset FROM a scenario
file."

**REWRITTEN (round 28, option C), because the original cannot be met by any
implementation:** *produce a chain-true dataset covering the states a scenario
can actually reach, with the states it cannot reach kept as hand-written
specimens and labelled as such.* The evidence for why is below and unchanged.

**Why it is impossible, not merely expensive** — three independent findings,
each re-verified rather than taken on a lens's word:

1. Today's DEMO describes states the realm cannot occupy. All 20 conviction
   values exceeded the realm's own maximum for their stated stake and lifetime —
   6x to 29x at the UNREACHABLE ceiling, 15x to 74x at the launch floor. Fixed in
   round 11 and now guarded by `scripts/check-demo-physics.py`.
2. Reaching DEMO's conviction honestly needs ~10.1M blocks, about twelve days of
   continuous mining for one court — and the result would then contradict DEMO's
   own timelines, which the 12-week dead-claim timeout forbids.
3. Three of DEMO's structures have NO chain source at all and never will:
   `desc` (the chain stores only a name), nested `folders` (chain folders are
   flat), and `relations` (the chain stores none). O6 assumed these already lived
   in the curation overlay; they do not, they live inside the literal.

**What both lenses recommended, independently:** split DEMO into a GENERATED
chain-true half and a HAND-WRITTEN overlay half. That split is a prerequisite for
either direction, and it is the piece deliberately not built, because building it
presumes the ruling.

**The three options, with what each costs and gives up:**

| option | what it means | cost | what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Split + dump | `make dump-demo` writes the chain-true half from a seeded node; `desc`/folders/relations stay hand-written | a working round | the demo's mature numbers: a seeded court is young, so conviction ~0, emission 0, no sparkline until a 120,960-block run |
| B. Split + check | keep DEMO hand-written; a checker holds it to the realm's arithmetic | mostly DONE — check-demo-physics.py already pins 5 invariants and found 2 real defects | nothing generated; drift is caught, not prevented |
| C. Rewrite the criterion | P5 becomes "produce a chain-true dataset covering the states a scenario CAN reach, with unreachable states kept as labelled hand-written specimens" | a wording change plus option A's work | honesty about what a demo is |

**THE THIRD OPTION, COSTED PROPERLY (round 29).** When I put this to the owner I
said a mature seed would take "a few hours". MEASURED, that is wrong by about
four times. Two deep runs each turned 2,200 mining transactions into ~4,420
blocks (2.01 blocks/tx, because tm2 adds a proof block whenever the app hash
changed) at 73-79 transactions a minute. Reaching the 120,960 blocks that a
trailing-week average needs therefore takes ~60,000 transactions, i.e.
**12.7-13.7 HOURS** of continuous mining, not a few.

And it buys less than it sounds like. 120,960 blocks is ONE emission period: it
gets the docket sparkline back and the first non-zero reservoir, but conviction
after one week is 0.6375% of stake at the launch floor — still nowhere near
today's sample, which needs ~10.1M blocks and contradicts its own timelines
anyway. So the honest menu is a sparse-but-true demo in twenty minutes, or a
slightly-less-sparse-but-true demo in fourteen hours. There is no version of
this that reproduces the sample.

**My recommendation: C, then A.** B is already most of the way built and has paid
for itself twice; A without C would leave the phase permanently failing against a
criterion no implementation can meet.
